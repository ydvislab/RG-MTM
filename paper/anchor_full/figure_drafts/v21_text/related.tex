\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Temporal Overviews of Spatial Data}
Animation keeps the spatial view of each instant, whereas static overviews expose distant times at once; which serves better depends on the task~\cite{Tversky:2002:Animation,Robertson:2008:Animation,Archambault:2011:AMM}, and generalized space--time cubes describe the operations that lead from one to the other~\cite{Bach:2017:STC}. A static overview must compress space into one display axis. Hovm\"oller diagrams average the field across a chosen direction~\cite{Hovmoller:1949}; dense pixel displays, geographic orderings and space-filling curves linearize the domain~\cite{Keim:2000:PIX,Guo:2006:SpatialOrder,Dafner:2000:CSFC,Rauscher:2025:1DO,Rauscher:2026:VB}, and volume lines and data-driven curves do so for volumes~\cite{Weissenboeck:2019:DVL,Zhou:2021:DSF}. For moving entities, one-dimensional projections and visual summaries such as MotionRugs, stable summaries, MoReVis and GroupRugs order objects by position or stability~\cite{Franke:2021:1DP,Buchmueller:2019:MR,Wulms:2021:SVS,Valdrighi:2024:MRV,Stolk:2025:GR}. Every such linearization distorts some spatial relation~\cite{Nonato:2019:MP,Aupetit:2007:VD,Nguyen:2013:FGV}. These displays keep a spatial coordinate but no feature hierarchy; we keep the merge tree and quantify what the coordinate then costs.

\subsection{Topology of Time-Varying Scalar Fields}
Merge and contour trees summarize how level sets connect, and persistence-based simplification controls their size~\cite{Heine:2016:Survey,Edelsbrunner:2002:TPS,Carr:2004:SFI,Tierny:2012:GTS,Tierny:2018:TTK}. Features are tracked over time by overlap or attribute similarity~\cite{Samtaney:1994:Tracking,Silver:1997:Tracking,Saikia:2017:GFT} and drawn as tracking graphs or nested tracking graphs~\cite{Widanagamaachchi:2012:DTG,Lukasczyk:2017:NTG,Lukasczyk:2020:DNTG}; time-varying merge trees are computed, compared and summarized with edit, Wasserstein and geometry-aware interleaving distances and sketches~\cite{Oesterling:2017:TVMT,Wetzels:2022:BDI,Pont:2022:WMT,Yan:2022:GAMT,Li:2023:SMT,Yan:2021:Survey}, and such pipelines follow cyclones, atmospheric rivers and flames~\cite{Yan:2024:TROPHY,Lan:2024:AR,Bremer:2010:Burning}. Labelled interleaving distances~\cite{Munch:2019:LINF,Gasparovic:2024:IID,Morozov:2013:IDMT} and ordered merge trees~\cite{Beurskens:2025:OMT,Beurskens:2025:ParkView} underlie the merge error we bound. Tracking graphs show how features appear, split and merge but not where they are; merge tree maps, discussed next, also show the values and nesting of every frame.

\subsection{Drawing Hierarchies in One Dimension}
Topological landscapes and their one-dimensional profiles draw a merge tree as terrain or a curve whose minima are the extrema and whose barriers are the merge levels~\cite{Weber:2007:TL,Oesterling:2013:TLP}, with geometry-preserving and temporally coherent variants~\cite{Beketayev:2012:GPTL,Herick:2020:TCTL}; contour trees also have planar drawings, joint layouts and treemap forms~\cite{Heine:2011:DCT,Lohfink:2020:FCT,Kamakshidasan:2021:MM}. Temporal treemaps place evolving hierarchies along time~\cite{Koepp:2019:TT}. TMTM aligns the profiles of successive merge trees into one static map~\cite{Koepp:2023:TMTM}, and ST-MTM adds relative spatial distances and temporal stability~\cite{STMTM:2026}. Storyline layouts face a related problem for groups of entities, trading crossings and wiggles against group contiguity~\cite{Tanahashi:2012:Storyline,Liu:2013:SF,Dobler:2025:Wiggle}. TMTM and ST-MTM are the only methods that produce the output we study, a static map of merge tree profiles over time, so they are our direct baselines; Table~\ref{tab:family} places them with tree-only orderings and space-only linearizations in one formulation, and Section~\ref{sec:experiments} measures all of them.

\subsection{Hierarchies against an External Order}
The conflict we bound recurs wherever a tree must respect an outside order. Optimal leaf ordering of dendrograms and clustered heat maps chooses a tree-consistent order for adjacent similarity~\cite{BarJoseph:2001:OLO,Eisen:1998:CA}, and reordering toward a target finds the closest tree-consistent permutation~\cite{Bulteau:2022:RT}. Spatially ordered treemaps and data-spatial grid maps trade hierarchy or data layout against geographic position~\cite{Wood:2008:SOT,vanBeusekom:2023:DSL}, much as cartograms trade shape against area~\cite{Nusrat:2016:CART}. Geophylogenies connect leaf orders to map locations~\cite{Klawitter:2025:GP}, and tanglegrams minimize crossings or displacement between two trees~\cite{Fernau:2010:CTC,Huson:2026:DOT,Dobler:2026:BCT}. Separation constraints keep laid-out objects disjoint~\cite{Dwyer:2006:IPSEP,Li:2019:SOI}, ultrametric fitting under the maximum norm relates to our optimal barriers~\cite{Farach:1995:RM,Chepoi:2000:LIA}, and merge distortion and topology-preserving projections measure how much hierarchy a mapping keeps~\cite{Eldridge:2015:MD,Doraiswamy:2021:TM,Rieck:2015:PH}. These works optimize crossings, sums or stress for a given instance; we give lower bounds, in closed form for points, on the worst displacement that every tree-consistent layout must accept, together with the merge error that removes it.
