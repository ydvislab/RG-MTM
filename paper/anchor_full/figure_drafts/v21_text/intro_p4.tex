We answer these questions in three steps. First, we write merge tree maps as one layout problem: a leaf order, intervals and anchors that minimize a weighted sum of positional, geometric and temporal terms under a merge-error allowance. TMTM and ST-MTM are its exact-topology instances without a spatial reference term, and projections and space-filling curves sit at its other end. Second, we bound what every instance must pay. Three lower bounds give the displacement that exact topology, finite widths and a bounded merge error force; they depend only on the tree, the projected locations and the widths, so they measure any map, name the merges that cause the displacement and the merge error that removes it, and split the error of a map into a part forced by the tree and a part chosen by its layout. Third, AnchorMTM, \emph{Spatially Anchored Merge Tree Maps}, is the instance with a reference term and one control, a merge-error budget $\varepsilon$, that approaches the bounds. Exact Mode ($\varepsilon=0$) keeps the tree, as TMTM and ST-MTM do; Budgeted Mode trades a declared and verified merge error for position.
