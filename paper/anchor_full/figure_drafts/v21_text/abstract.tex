\abstract{Temporal merge tree maps show in one image how the topology of a time-varying scalar field evolves, but their display axis has no fixed spatial meaning: a feature's position cannot be read as its location, and a feature that moves need not move on the map. Anchoring the axis to a spatial direction conflicts with the merge tree, because an exact profile keeps every subtree contiguous while the projected locations of sibling subtrees can interleave. We ask how much displacement this conflict forces. We describe merge tree maps as one layout problem, in which TMTM and ST-MTM are exact-topology instances without a spatial reference, and prove three lower bounds on the displacement that exact topology, finite feature widths and a bounded merge error force on every instance. The bounds need no layout, so they measure any map and split its error into a part forced by the tree and a part chosen by its layout. AnchorMTM approaches the bounds with a single control, a merge-error budget. The bounds equal the exhaustive optimum on controlled trees and are attained on 606 of 614 planar frames, and 31--95\% of the order error of TMTM and ST-MTM is not forced by the tree. At the same zero merge error, AnchorMTM lowers the mean worst displacement by 73--96\% on the planar ST-MTM benchmark and by 63--67\% on two volumes; a declared budget lowers it further, while ST-MTM's published neighborhood preservation remains higher.}
